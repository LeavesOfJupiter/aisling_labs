Revenant Syndrome

Abstract

Revenant Syndrome, as it was then called, was first observed in the mountainous regions of Central and Southern Europe. Originally described in the field reports of one Dr. Josephine Parker (henceforth refered to as Subject 01), early patients tended to appear in populations in close proximity to cave systems with a notable local presence of Plecotus macrobullaris.

Initial reports of the disease led to a presumtion that Revenant Syndrome was a new strain of Rabies, but vaccination efforts had little to no effect on patients.

Early symptoms included extreme photosensitivity and photophobia, odd compulsive behaviours surrounding counting small objects, distortion or unexplained absence of the image in reflective surfaces and photography, and an unwillingness to cross bodies of water. After progression, violent behaviours were observed, most notably compulsions toward hemophagia and cannibalism.
